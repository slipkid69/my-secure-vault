Madame Nightshade Went Out on the Pull

It was 9:00 PM—well past bedtime for most—but not for Madam Nightshade. Not, tonight she’d powdered her nose, polished her pearls, and strapped on orthopedic wedges with enough spring to out-jump a jazzercise instructor.

She entered Club Prunefunk like a disco hurricane, fog machine on standby, glitter cannon holstered. The bouncer—a centenarian with a hip replacement and a monocle—saluted her with reverence.

“I’m out to pull tonight,” she announced, cracking her knuckles like she was revving a bingo engine. “No weak tea. I want a man who remembers ration books and flirts like it’s 1942.”

Her first target was Gerald—age 98, moustache like a walrus, mobility scooter fitted with underglow lights. She offered him a Werther’s Original and winked so hard her eyelash detached.

“Are you my war bond? Because I’m ready to invest!” she purred.

He fainted. A success.

Next, she tangoed past two pensioners debating the morality of Jell-O, twirled beside a confused chap in slippers who just came in for soup, and made direct eye contact with the DJ—a man so old his playlist was entirely on wax cylinders.

“Play me something naughty,” she demanded. “Like Bing Crosby, but make it filthy.”

As the music swelled, she locked eyes with Lionel—104 years young, a twinkle in his cataracts. “You look like you’ve got a good pension and a bad attitude,” she whispered.

They danced. Bones clicked. Knees wobbled. Someone’s false teeth flew and landed in a piña colada.

By 1:00 AM, she’d pulled three gentlemen, two medical emergencies, and a conga line that lasted 14 feet before collapsing into a game of nap-themed Twister.

She left the club victorious, glitter in her hair, dentures in her purse, and a phone number scrawled on a napkin in Morse code.

Madam Nightshade: Still got it. Still pulling. Still terrifying.
