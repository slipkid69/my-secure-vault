Chapter I - Overview

Effluvia or Ophidian 369 is three times the Earth's diameter and about three times the Earth's mass, with a circumference nearly three times that of the Earth.

The total surface area of Ophidian 369 is about 591 million square miles, and sixty-nine per cent of the surface is water. Thirty billion Ophites are living on the planet. The Effluvian Sun is a yellow dwarf.

When humans visit Effluvia or take a pilgrimage or holiday there, they undergo rigorous physical training to ensure they are strong enough to cope with the extra 3-g forces of the Ophidian gravitational field.

For some, the Ophites' written and spoken language proves problematic because they write and speak as the words sound according to the syllable of a word.

Ophites are two to three metres long when fully grown and have humanoid faces, arms, hands, and feet.

Ophite's vision is tetrachromatic. Meaning that the Ophite's sensory perception of colour is four-dimensional; for the Ophites, their vision combines four or more primary colours of the light spectrum. Ophites can see lots more colour than most humanoid species because they have four cone types in their retina. One advantage is that the Ophites can detect when someone is ill due to their shroud colour change.

Another benefit that the Ophite's vision gives them is seeing ultraviolet, infrared, and visible light. Light is electromagnetic radiation, and the Ophite's vision enables them to see the short and long ends and the visible light of that spectrum. Ophites can detect heat from any warm-blooded life form or structure that emits heat. The Ophites have a significant advantage in the dark.

Additionally, the Ophite's eyesight enables them to reflect fifty per cent of the light at night, picked up by their eyes back toward that light source. Ophites have a reflective layer behind the iris, giving them excellent eyesight at night. It is known as eyeshine and reflects the light in various colours: red, green, orange, yellow, and white. Ophite's eyes are a mixture of different colour combinations: red, green, blue, yellow, brown, black, orange, purple, and others.

The humanoids call Effluvia, Ophidian 369. Effluvia is a watery tropical arboreal planet with large trees, plants, and vegetation. Ophites have a considerable symbiosis with the flora and, in some ways, are similar to a butterfly and flower's symbiotic relationship. As a butterfly feeds on a flower's nectar, it travels to another flower and pollinates it. Flowers are brightly coloured, and butterflies are attracted to them. A beautiful symbiotic relationship between different species.

Ophites have nostrils and a nose similar to other humanoids used for picking up many different aromas. Yet, Ophites can also use their tongue to significantly enhance their olfactory sense when desired; this aids them in their symbiotic relationship with many plants. Ophites can, like butterflies, feed on the nectar of radiant flowers, pollinate other flowers, and help develop new plant species.

The Ophite's tongue is similar to how a snake uses its tongue. A snake's tongue has receptors that pick up tiny chemical flecks; the Ophites perceive these particles as a scent like a snake. When the Ophites retract the tongue, an enzymatic report relays that information to the brain. The Ophites can store nectar in exocrine glands below the brain and behind their ears.

The Ophites believe that after the high priest has communed with God Almighty, the mountains will flow with sweet wine, and the land will flow with milk and life-giving waters. These things are symbolic blessings from God, pointing toward the glorious future that awaits them as they become spiritually revived, renewed, nourished and restored.

The Ophites believe that the pentagram shape created by the six holy mountains breathes out from their crest Almighty God Khashmals Holy Spirit. Thus, the Holy Spirit comes to burn within each of the Ophites as a bellowed spirit fire. Almighty God Khashmal's Holy Spirit permeates the whole of Effluvia for six days and nights. Thus, spiritually, mentally, and physically, bringing about well-being amongst the Ophites only after the high priest has fully completed his journey, fully communicating with Almighty God Khashmal on behalf of all Ophites across Effluvia.

Additionally, the Ophites believe that when the holy mountains breathe out Almighty God Khashmal's Holy Spirit, it also generates within all Ophites an increase in their justice, mercy, wisdom, understanding, and, above all, love. Therefore, for those ten days and ten nights, the crests of the six holy mountains remain connected by a filmy-coloured electrum pentagram of gold and silver as the fiery red spirit of fire reaches high up into the heavens, representing purity coupled with profound enlightenment from within and without. Sabaoth, Lord Of Hosts, the great eagle, appears above the pentagram.

Tetramorph

Aba Day Loth and Sabaoth

Aba Day Loth is the foundational individual of Ophite history. He is described as the first Ophite to reach true enlightenment and pass beyond the material universe. His achievement becomes the model around which the entire religion is constructed. The other Ophites do not merely admire him as a teacher. They study his body, his coat, his head and his divine qualities as though his transformed form contains a complete map of salvation.

His body remains serpent-like, representing caution, stealth, cunning and ingenuity. His head becomes leonine, representing strength, courage, honour, intrepidness and fertility. His brilliant white mother-of-pearl wool coat represents purity, tenderness, gentleness, humility, submission and forgiveness. The three-part structure of body, coat and head is recorded in Math-Wet 123, and these three elements become a doctrinal anatomy of spiritual perfection.

After his transfiguration, Khashmal renames him Sabaoth, Lord of Hosts. Eight days later he is resurrected and reunited with Khashmal as a drop within the infinite ocean of divine wisdom. Sabaoth is therefore both an individual Ophite and an elevated divine intermediary. He becomes the great eagle, the firstborn son of creation and the being through whom the Ophites communicate with Khashmal. He peers into their hearts, refines their minds and liquefies their understanding with divine wisdom.

Sabaoth is not simply a character who appears and disappears. He is the theological bridge connecting the material Ophite world with Khashmal. He appears above the pentagram during the blessings, instructs Za’a’zel and remains the model of what the Ophites hope to become.

Achamoth

Achamoth is the most intellectually expansive named Ophite. She is called the creative mother of wisdom and leads the Torch Dragons. She has built her house upon seven pillars, represented by seven apostles from among the Torch Dragons. Her voice is presented as an invitation to wisdom, happiness and truth, but her route to those things is highly elaborate and technologically occult.

She studies all the Ophite schools and attempts to merge their approaches into one. She does not remain within a single tradition. She investigates meditation, nature, mathematics, altered states, ritual, divination and the different doctrines of enlightenment. Her great amber sensory-deprivation lungs, the HIKE expeditions, ketamine, the Golden Bowl, the P.E.T., B.R.A.I.N.S. and Polynucleotide data shards all belong to her programme of visionary research.

During her first HIKE, Achamoth sees crystalline gold and silver, a sapphire throne, amber flames, lightning, a rainbow and the three faces of a serpent, lion and eagle. She becomes liquefied into an amber-coloured pool of gold and silver electrum, with the divine spark burning within her waters. She interprets the experience as an expansion of consciousness and evidence of spiritual progress.

Her most mature development comes after centuries of study. She spends ninety years examining nature and another ninety years travelling and debating with other Ophites. She eventually recognises that different individuals may pursue different paths while still contributing to a common good. She comes to understand Agape, the highest form of divine love, and sees variety among the Ophites as compatible with unity. Achamoth is therefore both a synthesiser of occult systems and the closest figure the story has to a philosopher of pluralism.

Za’a’zel

Za’a’zel is a Samphire high priest who takes upon himself the burdens of the Ophite people every nine years. His pilgrimage is one of the most detailed ceremonies in the story. He travels for ten days and ten nights towards the six holy mountains, eventually reaching Mount Islove and the Holy Temple of Renovation and Restoration.

His journey is an emptying process. He casts aside belief, unbelief, dogma, reason, worldly knowledge, desires and attachments. His identity gradually becomes a vessel for the Holy Spirit. He crosses nine valleys flowing with the waters of Shiloah, and each stage represents a further release of the self. His red rose has ten petals, one unfolding for each day and night of the pilgrimage.

At Mount Islove he partakes of holy bread and wine, bathes in the waters of Shiloah, receives the garments of glory and is anointed with holy oil. His inner coat is pure white silk. Over it he wears an azure robe reflecting heavenly things, with a purple, blue and scarlet belt decorated with pomegranates and golden bells. He gathers pomegranates, branches and leaves from the sacred trees before approaching the Haoma tree.

Za’a’zel is less a warrior than a ritual vessel. His character is defined by surrender, purification, endurance and ceremonial obedience. He carries the burden of the people by allowing his own identity to be emptied and reconstructed through the ritual.

Sabathiel

Sabathiel is Za’a’zel’s high priest brother. He guards the entrance to the holy pentagram and prepares the bread and wine that Za’a’zel receives before approaching Mount Islove. His role is protective and liturgical. He stands at the threshold between the ordinary world and the sacred centre.

Later, Sabathiel remains with Oduduwa, Kolkhis and Echidna to guard the temple while Za’a’zel remains there for thirty days and thirty nights. He is therefore one of the custodians of the ritual process, responsible for protection, preparation and maintaining the boundary around the sacred space.

Echidna

Echidna is the high priestess responsible for preparing Za’a’zel’s garments of glory. She helps him dress and adorn himself after his purification in the waters of Shiloah. Her role may appear practical, but it is symbolically important. She prepares the external form through which Za’a’zel becomes publicly recognisable as a sanctified high priest.

She also provides the wine, milk and Apas water used during the ceremony. Echidna therefore participates in both the bodily and ceremonial preparation of Za’a’zel. She is a figure of ritual craftsmanship, adornment and controlled transformation.

Kolkhis

Kolkhis is the high priest who stands near the mercy seat with the Book of Life. He is present at the decisive stage of Za’a’zel’s pilgrimage and acts as one of the senior authorities within the Holy Temple of Renovation and Restoration.

Kolkhis also performs the alchemical transmutation of the nine coloured leaves, wine, milk and Apas water into consecrated holy anointing oil. This gives him a role that combines priesthood, sacred chemistry and occult transformation. The story describes the process as a movement in which what is above transforms what is below, and what is outside transforms what is within.

Kolkhis therefore represents the institutional power to convert natural substances into a substance of spiritual authority. He is one of the figures who turns the beautiful symbolism of the Ophites into an organised ritual technology.

Oduduwa

Oduduwa is the high priest, Warrior King of Light and emissary of the Holy Temple of Renovation and Restoration. He blesses Za’a’zel with the words “veil is cut” as Za’a’zel enters the temple. His role is therefore both martial and priestly. He is not merely a ceremonial official. He embodies authority, protection and divine kingship.

Oduduwa has six wings containing three-dimensional photonic crystals. He can manipulate their light frequencies so that they appear in different shades of lustrous black: cool black, ebony, charcoal, outer-space black, taupe, banded onyx, jet, red black and rose ebony. Alabaster surrounds the triangular photonic crystals, and gold is scattered across his wings and body.

He carries the Sceptre of the Nine in his right hand. The sceptre demonstrates his authority under Khashmal and carries the inscription: “Your throne, O God, is forever and ever. The sceptre of your Kingdom is a sceptre of uprightness.” Oduduwa is therefore a visual synthesis of warrior power, priestly office, light manipulation, sacred geometry and royal authority.

He also remains with Sabathiel, Kolkhis and Echidna to guard the temple while Za’a’zel undergoes his long period of restoration. Oduduwa is the guardian and public face of the sacred institution.

Nawkhawsh

Nawkhawsh is one of the four Tetramorph high priests and is specifically called the wise and cunning master of light. The story describes the Tetramorph priests as illuminated ones, light bearers and fire bringers. Nawkhawsh is the most individually characterised of the four.

He is associated with razor-sharp vision, vigilance and intelligent manipulation of light. He carries a bronze flagpole wrapped by a fiery bronze serpent, which represents purification. His presence connects the Tetramorph with the serpent symbolism that runs through the entire Ophite civilisation.

Shefeefone

Shefeefone is one of the four Tetramorph high priests. The story gives less individual biography for Shefeefone than for Nawkhawsh, but places Shefeefone within the collective role of illuminated watcher, light bearer and fire bringer.

Shefeefone wears the red and blue turban called the blossom flower. The turban carries the four faces of the Tetramorph: serpent, lion, eagle and ox. Shefeefone therefore participates in the symbolic merging of cunning, strength, spiritual vision and endurance.

Akshoob

Akshoob is another of the four Tetramorph high priests. Like Shefeefone, Akshoob is presented mainly through the collective identity of the Tetramorph order rather than through a separate biography.

Akshoob is one of the guardians of the temple’s light within the Great Wheel. The four priests each have four wings and wear the blossom flower turbans. Akshoob’s role is therefore one of guardianship, illumination and preservation of the sacred structure containing the tenth crystal sphere and the nine divine qualities.

Tsehfah

Tsehfah is the fourth of the four Tetramorph high priests. Tsehfah shares the order’s role as illuminated watcher, light bearer and fire bringer. The four faces on the turban establish the order’s symbolic identity, while the four wings give the priests a distinct celestial form.

Together with Nawkhawsh, Shefeefone and Akshoob, Tsehfah helps guard the Great Wheel and the Temple of the Tetramorph. The order’s sacred spring, the Dragons Spring, flows with water from Shiloah and is believed to cleanse and bless the Ophites with the nine divine attributes.

Thermuthis

Thermuthis is a Tetramorph high priestess. The story gives her a title and position but does not provide the same extended personal description given to Za’a’zel, Achamoth or Oduduwa.

Her presence is nevertheless significant because the Tetramorph order is not exclusively male. She represents the female priestly authority within the order and belongs to the group responsible for guarding the Temple of the Tetramorph, the Great Wheel, the Dragons Spring and the mysteries of the divine attributes.

Kalos

Kalos is the Hierophant and counterpart to Thermuthis. He is described as beautiful through the purity of his love, reason and wisdom. His function is educational and interpretive. He helps the Ophites understand the mysteries of Khashmal’s divine attributes as outward signs of inward goodness.

Kalos wears a triple crown coloured green, blue and white. Green represents spiritual growth, white represents purity and blue represents heavenly things. His character is therefore built less around combat or visionary experience and more around reason, instruction, purity and the interpretation of divine mystery.

Principal Ophite schools and orders

The Samphire

The Samphire, or “burning ones”, are Ophites close to enlightenment. They wear white robes that form part of the Garments of Glory and possess golden energy fields that grow stronger as their knowledge, wisdom and understanding increase.

They can reflect Aba Day Loth’s divine qualities, although only as a dim radiance compared with him. Their six wings contain three-dimensional photonic crystals that can change colour and project three-dimensional images, including sound. They greet one another with “veil is cut”, a blessing referring to the removal of the veil between ignorance and enlightenment.

The Samphire are religious specialists, but they are also technological beings whose wings operate as photonic instruments. Their temples contain gold lampstands with lion heads and perpetual white flames, symbolising the burning away of the material shroud.

The Umbre Chi

The Umbre Chi mediate between neophytes and the Samphire. Their identity is expressed through the Alpha, Omega and Chi medallion. Alpha represents beginning, Omega represents ending, and Chi represents rebirth and resurrection. Alpha is enclosed within Omega, while Chi overlays Alpha.

They have four wings covering the body and two wings spreading upwards, making six wings in total. Their wings contain photonic crystals that reflect deep lapis lazuli and can change colour through manipulated light frequencies. Their eyes are deep lapis lazuli mixed with smoky gold, and looking into them produces a sense of mental clarity and contentment.

The Umbre Chi give neophytes Hevena Stones and Golden Ray flowers. They teach meditation, remove self-doubt and help individuals study the divine qualities mastered by Aba Day Loth. They are therefore pastoral guides and initiators.

The Sybarites

The Sybarites, also called sensualists, believe the material plane is the only reality that matters. They reject the spiritual programme of the Ophites and argue that if Khashmal exists, he is evil for creating a world of suffering.

Their speeches draw on the lament of Job. They ask why God gives light to those in misery, why existence is given to those with bitter souls and why beings are allowed to blossom only to be cut off. They argue that the Ophite desire for self-realisation may be a sadistic and impossible burden placed into their genetic makeup.

The Sybarites therefore represent materialist revolt, sensual freedom and resistance to occult or religious ascent. They say those who refuse the flesh are weak and afraid, while the story leaves open the counterargument that surrendering to desire may itself be a form of weakness. They are not simple villains. They are the school that exposes the cruelty and internal conflict of the official religious system.

M.A.S.K.S.

M.A.S.K.S. means Mind-Altering Spiritual Karma School. Its members use masks to conceal their ordinary identity and invite another spiritual Ophite persona or essence to take over.

They use Kava, transcendental meditation and pharmakeia to enter a euphoric trance. Once the wearer has surrendered sovereignty, a new personality may emerge. The wearer may become a king, queen, combined male and female persona, hermaphrodite personality or genderless being.

The masks are secretive and initiatory. They represent concealment, ritual transformation, altered identity and the invitation of another influence into the self. Unlike the Samphire, who burn away the material shroud through divine attributes, the M.A.S.K.S. attempt to replace or reshape the ordinary self through occult identity exchange.

The Honourable Suicide school

This school believes that neither belief in God nor sensual indulgence is the correct way. It rejects both the right-hand and left-hand paths and claims that ritual death alone will reveal the truth.

The story contains a deliberate dark joke in their doctrine. They admit that none of the people who have taken this path has returned to explain what they discovered. Their numbers are therefore dwindling, but they remain undeterred. Their statement that some give their time and others their possessions, while all the school asks is that one give one’s life, is a parody of ordinary religious fundraising and devotion.

They represent the terminal form of salvation through works. Their doctrine turns death into the final ritual achievement and makes itself impossible to test because its successful members cannot return to report whether it worked.

The Catalysts

The Catalysts are priests and priestesses who pursue enlightenment through the dissolution of the ego. They believe the parts of the shroud that burn away become eternal energy, called the Infinite Dynamic or I.D. waters.

They use the sweven, transcendental meditation, divination, crystal scrying and pharmakeia. The sweven produces dreams and visions which the Ophites interpret and record through drawings and paintings. They teach that consciousness is like turbulent water and that the neophyte must learn to calm the storm before attempting to walk upon the water.

Crystal scrying is applied to the forehead, heart and reproductive organs. The forehead reveals the developing consciousness, the heart reveals emotional development and the reproductive organs reveal whether the emerging persona will be male, female or a union of both. The Catalysts therefore treat identity as something that can be viewed, redirected and deliberately fashioned through occult technique.

Achamoth’s Torch Dragons

The Torch Dragons are the school founded and guided by Achamoth. They attempt to merge all the different routes to enlightenment into a single system.

Their amber “lungs” are sensory-deprivation chambers with triangular geometry, blue amber couches, red garnet feet and HIKE inscriptions. HIKE means hallucinogenic interdimensional ketamine expedition. The Torch Dragons use ketamine produced from plant nectar, nature sounds, ocean sounds, rain, heartbeats and psychedelic space music.

They record their experiences through the Golden Bowl, P.E.T., B.R.A.I.N.S. and Polynucleotide synthetic DNA data shards. The Torch Dragons represent the transformation of occult initiation into a technologically mediated system of altered consciousness and recorded revelation.

The Archimedeans

The Archimedeans are Ophites with an exceptional aptitude for mathematics and puzzles. They try to calculate Khashmal’s divine qualities and treat mathematical infinity as evidence of the divine mind.

They argue that infinite divine qualities can exist temporarily within finite mortal beings. Their snowflake example shows how they interpret natural mathematics as a testimony of divine intelligence. They are the school that attempts to reach God not through trance or sensuality, but through calculation, pattern, symmetry and intellectual astonishment.

The Tetramorph

The Tetramorph are a high priestly order built around four faces: serpent, lion, eagle and ox. These faces combine cunning, strength, spiritual vision and endurance. Their four high priests each have four wings and wear red and blue turbans called the blossom flower.

They guard the Temple of the Tetramorph, the Great Wheel, the tenth crystal sphere and the Dragons Spring. They call Khashmal Bythos, meaning the Profound One, and the Godhead of Light. The order is concerned with purification, illumination, sacred water and the preservation of divine knowledge.

The civilisation is therefore a cast of competing spiritual programmes embodied as characters, priesthoods and schools.
